💡 Health Tips

Prevent Before You
Need to Cure

Simple daily habits that protect your smile and vision for life.

🪥
Brush Twice Daily
Brush with fluoride toothpaste for 2 minutes, morning and night. Use soft bristles to protect enamel.
🧵
Floss Every Day
Flossing removes plaque between teeth that your brush can't reach — preventing gum disease and cavities.
💧
Stay Hydrated
Water washes away food particles and bacteria. Fluoridated water also strengthens your enamel naturally.
🍎
Eat Tooth-Friendly Foods
Crunchy fruits and vegetables clean teeth naturally. Limit sugary snacks and acidic drinks.
🚫
Avoid Tobacco & Alcohol
Tobacco causes gum disease, tooth decay, and oral cancer. Alcohol erodes enamel and dries the mouth.
📅
Regular Dental Checkups
Visit your dentist every 6 months for cleaning and early detection of any problems.
👶
Children's Early Care
Start dental visits by age 1. Early orthodontic evaluation can prevent complex treatments later.
👴
Senior Dental Health
Seniors need extra care. Dry mouth from medications increases cavity risk. We offer specialized geriatric dentistry.
💻
20-20-20 Rule
Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds to reduce digital eye strain.
☀️
UV Protection
Always wear UV-protective sunglasses outdoors. UV exposure is a major risk factor for cataracts.
🥦
Eat for Eye Health
Leafy greens, carrots, fish rich in omega-3, and citrus fruits support retinal health and reduce degeneration risk.
🚭
Quit Smoking
Smoking doubles the risk of cataracts and macular degeneration. It also damages the optic nerve.
🩸
Control Diabetes & BP
Diabetes causes diabetic retinopathy — the leading cause of blindness. Regular blood sugar control is essential.
👁
Annual Eye Exams
Many eye diseases like glaucoma have no early symptoms. Annual exams catch problems before vision is lost.
😴
Rest Your Eyes
Adequate sleep allows eyes to recover. Eye strain from screens can be managed with proper rest and lighting.
👧
Child Eye Screening
Preschool eye screening detects amblyopia early. Untreated lazy eye becomes permanent — early intervention is key.
